📖 What Is a Wheel?

🎟 What is a wheel?

A wheel is a carefully chosen list of tickets. Instead of picking numbers randomly, a wheel guarantees that certain number patterns always appear together somewhere in your ticket set — no matter which numbers come up.

🎱 Pool size (v)

The total count of numbers you are choosing from. For example, in a 6/49 lottery the pool is 49. Set this to whatever the rules of your game say.

🃏 Numbers per ticket (k)

How many numbers go on each ticket. In a 6/49 lottery that is 6. Every ticket the tool creates will have exactly this many numbers.

🏆 Guarantee level (t)

The size of the pattern you want to guarantee. If t=3, the wheel promises that for every group of 3 numbers in your pool, at least one of your tickets will contain all 3 of them.

Quick example: With v=10, k=3, t=2 you are saying: "I want 3-number tickets from a pool of 10, and I need every possible pair of numbers to appear on at least one ticket." There are C(10,2) = 45 pairs. The tool might cover all of them with as few as 5 tickets — far fewer than the 120 possible tickets!

🔬 How It Works (Plain English)

1
List all the patterns

The tool first figures out every possible t-number pattern in your pool. For pool=10, t=2 that is 45 pairs. For pool=49, t=3 that is 18,424 triples.

2
Pick the most useful ticket first

It scores every candidate ticket by how many uncovered patterns it contains, then picks the winner. This "most helpful first" approach is called greedy selection.

3
Repeat until everything is covered

Each picked ticket crosses its patterns off the list. The process repeats, always choosing the ticket that adds the most new coverage.

4
Try again from scratch (restarts)

To find a smaller wheel, the tool reruns the whole process with a different random shuffle each time and keeps the best (smallest) result.

5
Remove unnecessary tickets

At the end it checks whether any ticket is now redundant — its patterns are all covered by other tickets. Redundant tickets are removed to shrink the wheel.

6
Large jobs use smart sampling

When there are millions of possible tickets (like 6/49), testing every one each round would freeze the browser. Instead the tool randomly samples a group of promising candidates per round. The result is still a very good wheel — just not guaranteed to be the absolute smallest possible.
